# NEXT25 Opportunity Brief™ — Depth Standard
## Product decision
Nine possibilities remain the expansion layer. The strongest 2–3 candidates receive a deeper Opportunity Brief so customers get practical implementation help where it matters.

## Required career/business sections
1 Why this surfaced — customer's relevant signals, answers and tensions.
2 What the career/business actually is.
3 What a typical week could contain.
4 Who pays / who benefits.
5 Entry models: employee, fractional, consulting, acquisition, startup, portfolio, teaching, etc. as relevant.
6 Transferable assets.
7 Gaps to investigate: skills, technology, credentials, licenses, insurance, proof.
8 Economics to research: pricing/compensation models, startup/recurring costs, time-to-revenue; never promise earnings.
9 Market reality using current sourced research when factual claims are made.
10 Regulatory/licensing screen for jurisdiction/industry.
11 Startup complexity with reasons.
12 First 30 days: 4–6 concrete actions.
13 90-Day Experiment™ with evidence criteria.
14 What could make it wrong: contradictions, aversions, constraints, failure modes.
15 Questions before committing.
16 Current resources/research appendix with an “as of” date.

## Language
Prefer “worth investigating,” “could fit because,” “one way to test,” “verify whether.”
Avoid “you should become,” “this will earn,” “guaranteed,” “perfect fit,” “best career.”

## Research rule
Time-sensitive claims about earnings, demand, licensing, startup costs, taxes, regulations, platforms or market conditions are researched at generation/review time and carry source/date metadata.

## Business addendum
Also cover customer problem/value proposition, narrow first offer, business-model alternatives, acquisition-channel tests, validation interviews, MVP/pilot, startup-cost categories, pricing hypotheses, professional questions and 90-day stop/continue criteria.
