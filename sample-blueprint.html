PERSONALIZED NEXT25 BLUEPRINT™ • SAMPLE

What could your
next chapter become?

Prepared for Alex Morgan — a fictional example created to show the experience and depth of a NEXT25 Blueprint.

SECOND LIFE ARCHITECT™NEXT25

HOW TO READ THIS SAMPLE

A Blueprint is not an instruction. It is a better field of view.

Alex is 64 and approaching a transition after a long professional career. Alex wants greater independence, meaningful intellectual engagement and more time for family and travel, but does not want to recreate a demanding full-time role. The following is illustrative—not a recommendation, prediction or validated assessment.

01

WHERE YOU ARE NOW

The transition is not simply from work to retirement.

Alex appears less interested in “stopping” than in gaining greater control over where experience, energy and time go next. The challenge is to preserve intellectual stimulation and usefulness without rebuilding the obligations Alex wants to leave behind.

“I want to wake up with something interesting to do—but I don’t want every day owned by somebody else’s calendar.”
02

WHAT NEXT25 NOTICED

Experience remains valuable. The desired container for it may be changing.

Autonomy

Greater control over time and commitments repeatedly appears.

Expertise

Alex does not want to discard decades of knowledge—only use it more selectively.

Contribution

Being useful still matters, particularly through teaching and guidance.

Exploration

Travel and learning are not “extras”; they are conditions the next chapter should leave room for.

03

TENSIONS & TRADEOFFS

Freedom and engagement both matter.

A completely unstructured retirement may provide freedom without enough engagement. A conventional consulting practice may provide engagement while gradually rebuilding the schedule Alex is trying to escape.

The design question is therefore not “work or retire?” It is “what level and form of useful engagement protects the freedom you want?”

04

NINE POSSIBILITIES WORTH EXPLORING

Three ways of widening the field.

STRONG FITFractional Expert Role

Selective use of expertise with bounded commitments.

STRONG FITBoard Adviser

Strategic contribution without operating responsibility.

STRONG FITPrivate Workshop Practice

Teach accumulated knowledge in concentrated formats.

ADJACENTMentor-in-Residence

Useful involvement centered on people rather than deliverables.

ADJACENTNiche Research Briefing

Turn curiosity and expertise into periodic insight.

ADJACENTAdvisory + Teaching Portfolio

Combine two lighter roles rather than choose one identity.

OVERLOOKEDOral History Producer

Apply interviewing and judgment in a new, legacy-oriented setting.

OVERLOOKEDLearning Sabbatical

Test whether structured learning is itself part of the next chapter.

OVERLOOKEDHeritage Journey Creator

Combine family, research, travel and storytelling.

These examples are illustrative hypotheses. Actual possibilities require investigation of feasibility, demand, qualifications, economics and personal experience.

05

YOUR POSSIBILITY GAP™

The best discovery may sit one step beyond the obvious answer.

Alex initially described the future as “some consulting and more travel.” The overlooked possibilities matter because they test whether the underlying needs—usefulness, curiosity, autonomy, family and exploration—can be satisfied in configurations Alex had not named.

06

90-DAY EXPERIMENTS™

Test the life before deciding the life.

Experiment A

One bounded advisory project

Accept one 4–6 week project with no direct reports. Track energy before, during and after each work session.

Experiment B

Teach once

Design one 60-minute workshop for a real audience. Notice whether preparation and delivery create energy or obligation.

Experiment C

Learning journey

Design a three-day trip around a subject Alex genuinely wants to understand. Ask whether structured exploration deserves a recurring place in life.

07

25 FOR 25™

Life is larger than work.

Alex's list might include: take grandchildren on individual trips; learn documentary photography; teach a course; spend a month living somewhere new; record family stories; advise one mission-driven organization; learn conversational Italian; create an annual friends' expedition; write ten essays worth keeping.

08

QUESTIONS STILL WORTH ANSWERING

A good Blueprint should create better questions.

Which possibility would still appeal on an ordinary Tuesday? What amount of scheduled responsibility makes life richer rather than smaller? Is income a requirement, a scorecard, or simply welcome? Which part of Alex's former identity deserves to continue—and which part has earned the right to end?

09

YOUR NEXT 90 DAYS

Move from imagination to evidence.

Days 1–30

Investigate. Speak with people actually doing the work. Refine assumptions.

Days 31–60

Experiment. Complete one bounded version in the real world.

Days 61–90

Review. What created energy? What created friction? What would you voluntarily repeat?
