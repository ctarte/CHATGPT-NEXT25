# Income, Customer & Employer Research Standard
## Income
Career paths: research current national and, when relevant, state/metropolitan wage distributions, education/experience and outlook. In the U.S., use BLS OOH/OEWS when the occupation maps cleanly; supplement with authoritative licensing and credible current industry sources. Show source, geography, data year/as-of date, measure and limitations. Employee wage data is not consulting/business income.

Business/self-employment: do not state one expected-income number as fact. Model transparent scenarios: Revenue = customers × average price × frequency. Separately identify costs so revenue is not confused with profit or owner income. Low/Illustrative/Higher-activity scenarios require explicit editable assumptions and must be labeled scenarios, not forecasts.

## Where to find customers
Every business Opportunity Brief names 3–6 realistic acquisition channels tailored to the buyer: warm referrals; associations; defined-buyer direct outreach; referral partnerships; speaking/teaching/events; relevant marketplaces/directories; content/search/community; government procurement when relevant. For each explain who is there, why it fits, first action, friction and evidence to collect. Use current research.

## Where to find employers
Every career Opportunity Brief includes employer types/industries, geographic/remote constraints, associations, relevant niche/general job platforms, target-company method, alumni/former-colleague network, recruiters when appropriate, direct employer career pages and public-sector sources where relevant. Include 5–10 job-title/search variants.

## Evidence
“Could earn” is not “will earn.” State population, geography and time period. Business revenue, owner compensation, profit and employee wages are different measures and must not be blended.
