SECOND LIFE ARCHITECT™ — DEEP QUESTIONNAIRE DESIGN DIRECTION

The production questionnaire should move beyond generic retirement questions. The next build should expand the assessment across these domains:

1. Transition & Current Chapter — retirement, career change, burnout, first business, general reinvention.
2. Energy & Burnout — what drains, restores, excites, bores, and creates anticipation.
3. Deferred Dreams — ideas postponed by family, finances, timing, peer pressure, confidence, lack of direction.
4. Experience & Expertise — accumulated knowledge, teachable skills, consulting, mentoring, writing, speaking.
5. Entrepreneurship & Independence — first business, solo practice, lifestyle business, acquisition, online/service/product models.
6. Curiosity & Creativity — subjects researched voluntarily, making, writing, design, technology, history, travel, invention.
7. Freedom & Constraints — time, money, caregiving, property, geography, energy, commitments, risk tolerance.
8. Lifestyle & Experiences — ideal pace, ordinary-day design, travel, learning, family, adventure, solitude.
9. People & Relationships — spouse, family, friends, collaborators, community, intellectual peers, independence.
10. Meaning & Contribution — helping, solving, creating, teaching, building, serving, mastery, exploration.
11. Organization & Continuity — household knowledge, documents, contacts, digital life, property, business continuity.
12. Legacy & What Endures — stories, values, traditions, expertise, creative work, philanthropy, family continuity.

ANSWER-DESIGN PRINCIPLES
- Include conventional and overlooked possibilities.
- Do not force retirement as the assumed goal.
- Separate burnout from lack of purpose.
- Distinguish “leave my old career behind” from “use my expertise differently.”
- Include first-time entrepreneurship and small/lifestyle business models.
- Ask what gives energy, not merely what someone is good at.
- Ask about constraints and desired lifestyle before suggesting opportunities.
- Use “select any” answers plus open reflections to reveal combinations.
- Surface possibilities, not prescriptions.
- Use 90-day experiments to test fit before major commitments.
- Avoid implying a single perfect career, business, or life choice.

The Blueprint engine should eventually combine these responses into archetype clues, opportunity signals, constraints, fit filters, overlooked possibilities, 25 for 25 priorities, and 90-Day Experiments.
